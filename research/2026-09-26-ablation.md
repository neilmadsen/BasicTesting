# Ablation: which decisions lose value to Forge's AI

Follow-up to [the threat-order note](2026-09-25-threat-order.md). There, Jev with a static plan placed
+0.46 worse than Forge's AI on the same 48 seeded games (paired standard error 0.19), and blind audits
of its overrules kept favouring it. That conflict raised a question: where does the value leak?

Each arm here lets Jev decide only some decision kinds (`EDH_PILOT_KINDS`) and leaves the rest to Forge.
All arms use a static plan (no Claude calls) and the same 48 games: seed 11, ten pods, per-game
reseeding. Each is compared game by game with the Forge-only run of those seeds (`crn-forge48`) using
`research/paired.py`. Data: `2026-09-26-ablation-data/`.

Notes on the table:
- **Positive = worse than Forge.** Place 1 = won, 4 = first out.
- **Games** counts games without a draw in either arm. Draws are dropped, so the Forge column moves
  slightly.
- **"The rest"** covers mulligans, searches, confirms (mostly shock-land life payments), sacrifices
  and their costs, discards, trigger targets, and optional triggers.
- **Cost per overrule** is the difference times the games, divided by the overrules (answers that
  replaced Forge's), with its standard error. Only overrules can change a game. When Jev's answer
  matches Forge's, or its preference doesn't clear the margin gate, Forge's action is played.

## What it shows

**Every kind Jev decides costs placement.** Every point estimate in the table is positive. Taken
alone, only combat (2.2 standard errors) and actions (1.9) are at or near conventional significance. No single
kind accounts for the whole deficit.

**Blocks are close to neutral.** At +0.09 ± 0.10, blocks are the one kind that plausibly costs
nothing.
- This corrects my earlier observational reading, which blamed blocks. That reading was confounded,
  most likely because we block more in games we are already losing.
- The blind audit's 15–2 in favour of Jev's blocks is consistent with this, and may even be right.

**Attack overrules are the costliest each.** Each costs about a tenth of a place.
- They are rare, about 1.9 a game, but large; an attack or hold decides a whole combat.
- Action overrules are the reverse: 17 a game at about 0.02 each, for a similar total.
- **Actions are the biggest lever by volume.**

**The parts add to more than the whole.**
- Actions + combat + the rest come to +0.92, against +0.46 for Jev deciding everything.
- The gap is about 1.3 standard errors, so it may be noise.
- If it is real, there is an explanation for it: **Forge's AI is coherent with itself.** Its main-phase
  logic holds back creatures and mana for its own combat and response logic, and its attack logic
  assumes its own blocks. Replacing any one part breaks that coupling. Replacing all of them doesn't
  break it much further.
- On that reading, much of the cost isn't the individual decisions being bad, which is what the blind
  audits check and why they favour Jev. The cost is Jev's decisions and Forge's remaining ones not
  fitting together.
- I can't separate these readings from this data.

**Pressure doesn't separate the arms any more.**
- Attackers sent (3.4–4.2 a game) and combat damage dealt (9–13) are close to Forge's (3.7 and 11.9)
  in every arm, including the full static arm (3.9 and 12.1). The passive-attack problem from the
  fast-loop note is fixed.
- Median damage taken is 20–24 in every arm.
- The means (28–77) are dominated by one or two infinite-loop games per arm. One game logged 2,216
  damage. Read the per-game means in the scorecard's pressure lines with that in mind.

## Method notes

- **Common random numbers work when arms differ in few decisions.**
  - Blocks-only and attacks-only pairs stay identical for a median of 26–35 turns, with place
    correlations of 0.6–0.8. That halves the standard error compared with independent samples.
  - The actions-only and full arms diverge after about 4 turns, and pairing buys nothing.
  - So for small executor changes, a paired 48-game run resolves about 0.25 places. For large
    changes, it resolves about 0.4.
- **All arms share one baseline.** Every difference is against the same Forge run, whose games are
  about average for this deck: 2.52 here, and 2.60 in the old-seeding run. If that run was lucky or
  unlucky, every row shifts by the same amount. The comparisons between rows don't depend on it.
- **Mulligans come first.** In the rest-arm, 4 of 43 pairs diverged before turn 1, because
  mulligans come first.

## What follows: steer mode

If Jev's tactical decisions lose value mostly through the coupling explanation, then better
per-decision judgement won't fix them. The fix is to overrule Forge only for a reason Forge can't see.
That is `--steer`, committed in 999fa41. In steer mode, Forge keeps its tactics. Jev's answer stands
only when it differs from Forge's for a reason taken from the memo:
- the planned play this turn;
- the memo's #1 threat;
- lethal;
- a HOLD card;
- X sizing;
- the hold question.

Every other difference goes back to Forge's pick.

Replaying 1,200 logged decisions from the 48-game Opus arm (threat order, strategist every 3rd turn)
under steer:
- 237 of 1,488 answers change, and 235 of those changes go back to Forge's pick.
- About half of the changes are actions (122), then searches (32) and attacks (20).

This matches the ablation: most of what Jev overrules on its own judgement goes.

## Next

1. **Sanity arm: static plan + `--steer`, 48 games, paired.** With no memo there are almost no reasons
   to overrule, so this should land at Forge's level. That checks steer mode doesn't damage anything
   by itself. Jev only, about 30 minutes.
2. **The real test: Opus every 3rd turn + threat order + `--steer`, 48 games, paired with
   `crn-forge48`.**
   - It asks whether the strategist's direction is worth anything once the tactics are Forge's.
   - The full pilot with the same strategist reached parity (2.70 against 2.60, unpaired) while
     carrying an executor that costs about 0.46. If strategy and tactics add up, this arm should beat
     Forge by a margin this sample can see.
   - About 250–400 Opus calls and 2–3 hours.
3. **Where to look if steer mode works.** The per-overrule costs say where Jev's own judgement is worth
   re-admitting, one kind at a time, with the same paired design:
   - blocks first, which are neutral already;
   - then trigger targets and searches.
