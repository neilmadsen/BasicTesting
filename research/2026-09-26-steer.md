# Steer mode, and why overruling Forge costs

Follow-up to [the ablation note](2026-09-26-ablation.md). That note found that every kind of decision
Jev takes over from Forge's AI loses placement, and it proposed steer mode:
- Forge keeps the tactics.
- Jev overrules only when the memo gives a reason Forge can't see.

All runs below use the same 48 games (seed 11, per-game reseeding) and are paired with the Forge-only run
of those games (`crn-forge48`, average place 2.52). Data: `2026-09-26-steer-data/`.

## Results

| arm | avg place | vs Forge (paired) | overrules / game |
|---|---|---|---|
| steer, static plan | 2.58 | +0.06 ± 0.14 | 0.7 |
| steer, Opus every 3rd turn + threat order | 2.91 | **+0.39 ± 0.17** | 19 |
| Jev decides actions, Forge's after-combat timing kept | 2.81 | +0.29 ± 0.17 | 9.9 |
| Jev decides attacks, with crack-back info | 2.62 | +0.10 ± 0.10 | 1.6 |
| *(ablation) Jev decides actions* | 2.85 | +0.37 ± 0.19 | 15.2 |
| *(ablation) Jev decides attacks* | 2.71 | +0.19 ± 0.13 | 1.9 |

Positive means worse than Forge. Place 1 = won, 4 = first out.

### Steer mode costs nothing by itself

With a static plan there is almost nothing to overrule. The arm lands level with Forge.

So the pilot's plumbing costs nothing measurable:
- the questions it asks at every decision;
- its replacement for Forge's cost-payment logic;
- clearing Forge's proposed blocks and putting them back.

The ablation's losses come from the overrules themselves.

That arm also exposed a flaw. Steer mode treated every X value and every "keep mana open" decision as a
reason from the memo, so Jev was sizing X on its own judgement: 29 of 31 overrules were Walking Ballista
cast for X = 0. Fixed in ec1956b: an X value or a hold now needs the memo to name the card.

### Opus + steer loses to Forge

The Opus arm was clean: 264 memos, none failed. The threat order was followed: 90% of our attacks and 97%
of our removal hit the memo's #1 threat, against Forge's 73% and 72% on the same decisions.

It still placed +0.39 worse than Forge, and +0.30 ± 0.17 worse than steer on a static plan.

Of its 19 overrules a game, 14 were "planned play":
- the memo's THIS TURN names a card;
- Forge does something else;
- Jev plays the named card.

That is the actions-only ablation over again, at the same rate and the same cost. Planned plays are no
longer a steer reason (df59884).

### It isn't the timing

The single most common action overrule, in both the actions-only arm and the Opus arm, was casting in
main 1 a permanent Forge would cast after combat (6–7 a game).

A rule that kept Forge's timing:
- removed those overrules (5.5 deferrals a game);
- cut action overrules from 15.2 to 9.9 a game;
- changed placement by +0.00 ± 0.20 against the same arm without it.

It was reverted (32efa83).

### It isn't a baseline artifact

- **The worry:** a lucky Forge baseline would make every divergent arm regress toward its true mean, and
  look worse even with an equal policy.
- **Why not:** Forge has two independent 48-game samples on these pods, 2.52 and 2.60. The heavily
  overruling arms cluster near 2.85. The gap is real.

### Attacks: the audit

A subagent read every game of the attack-only arm whose place differed from Forge's (14 of 48). It started
at the exact point of divergence and read both timelines. The per-game audits don't do that.

**What the overrules were.**
- 64 of 90 attack overrules sent a creature Forge kept home, 51 of them at a player with no untapped
  blocker.
- The option text said the attacker was safe. It said nothing about what tapping it left at home.
- In the two rounds after such an overrule we took 189 damage, against 135 on the same games under Forge.

**Verdicts.**
- **Clearly caused by the overrule:** 3 games worse, 2 better, net 3 places lost.
  - Two of the three losses were crack-backs: Massacre Wurm sent while we were at 10 life, and Greenwarden
    sent into four 9/9 elves.
  - The third was aiming at a creatureless player at 9 life and sparing the Ur-Dragon player, who won.
- **Later divergence** (different draws and choices after trivial pokes): 7 games. The 5–2 split among
  them is noise.
- **Harness divergence, no overrule involved:** 2 games.

**Execution was sound.** Declared attacks matched Jev's choice for 521 of 522 creatures.

**The fix (df59884).** Jev's attack decisions now carry:
- `crack_back`: our life, each opponent's creature power, and a warning when one of them alone could kill
  us;
- a tag on the option that is Forge's own choice.

The rerun sent fewer creatures Forge held (64 → 25) and held more that Forge sent (8 → 33). It placed
+0.10 ± 0.10 against Forge, compared with +0.19 before. That is in the right direction but not
significant.

## What this says

**Overruling Forge's AI mostly costs placement, whoever does it.** That holds for Jev on its own
judgement, and for Jev carrying out an Opus plan. Each fix so far has either done nothing (timing) or
helped by less than 48 games can resolve (crack-back).

**The one mechanism found concretely is a missing-information blind spot, not bad judgement as such.**
Forge weighs things our option text didn't show, such as what an attack leaves at home.

That suggests a general reading of Forge's choices: they are evidence about facts the pilot can't see.
- The better executor move is to explain Forge's reasons, as `crack_back` and the Forge-pick tag do.
- It isn't to overrule Forge more cleverly.

**The strategist's value on top of Forge is still unmeasured.** The Opus arm's loss came from planned
plays. Its threat-order redirects were a small part of its overrules (about 3 a game) and can't be
separated in that arm.

## Next

1. **Opus + steer without planned plays, 48 games, paired.** Only threat-order redirects, lethal, and
   holds or X values for cards the memo names remain. This is the clean test of whether the strategist's
   direction adds anything to Forge's tactics. It costs about 260 Opus calls.
2. **If that also loses or ties**, the evidence says the pilot should not overrule Forge in this
   simulator. Opus's judgement is then better spent on deckbuilding and on reading the logs of Forge-only
   games.
