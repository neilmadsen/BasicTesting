# Can Jev (+ an LLM strategist) pilot Forge games better than Forge's AI?

**Short answer, first spike: not yet.** The architecture works and is cheap. The
pilot measurably changes how the deck is played. It did not produce better
outcomes in a 12-game-per-arm test, and the reason is mostly structural, not a
lack of intelligence.

## What was built

- **Java plug-in** (`pilot/src/edh/pilot`): extends Forge's AI controller with no
  fork of Forge. On our own main phases with an empty stack it collects:
  - Forge's pick;
  - every play Forge's evaluator approves;
  - land drops;
  - legal, payable plays Forge *declines*, targeted in Forge's mandatory mode and
    labelled with Forge's reason;
  - pass.

  It posts these with a compact board state to a localhost sidecar. Everything
  else stays with Forge: combat, blocks, instant-speed responses, cost choices
  and targets.
- **Executor (Jev):** one Choice question per decision. Its state is the deck
  brief plus pilot notes, the latest strategy memo and the board. It overrules
  Forge's pick only when its top choice beats it by a probability margin.
- **Strategist (Claude Opus 5.5 via lightweight headless `claude -p`):** once per
  turn of ours it writes a 120-word memo (PRIORITIES / THREAT / HOLD). The sim
  pauses for it, since a simulation can wait. This is the Doom-demo split
  (planner occasionally, executor per tick) without real-time pressure.

## Experiment

Deck: Muldrotha "Undertaker's Toolbox" (recurring-removal control), bracket 3.
Each arm ran the same pods, seats and seeds: 3 pods of 4 games against the B3
EDHREC gauntlet.

## What the logs show

- **Jev alone** overruled Forge mostly to make plays Forge's AI declines, such
  as Executioner's Capsule (cast and activated), Claws of Gix, the Baubles,
  Cryogen Relic and Seal of Primordium. It surfaced the engine cards Forge never
  casts (never-cast fell from 14 to 7). These look like reasonable plays taken
  one at a time. As a sequence they didn't win games.
- **With Opus 5.5 memos** the overrules look like a better player's priorities:
  - it cast Muldrotha earlier (11 overrules; first cast round 6.3 vs 7.2);
  - it sequenced fetch lands for the graveyard lane;
  - it deployed Seal of Doom, Binding the Old Gods and Baba Lysaga activations.

  The memos themselves are specific and correct in spirit. Example: "land lane
  = Verdant Catacombs from graveyard every turn; enchantment lane = Sinister
  Concoction … destroy Serra Ascendant first, Giada second".
- **Every arm dies around round 11 to combat damage** from go-wide boards
  (Y'shtola, Edgar, Giada). The pilot controls none of the decisions that
  decide those games: attacks, blocks, instant-speed interaction, and which
  permanent to sacrifice for a cost.

## Why it didn't win more (hypotheses, most likely first)

1. **Control surface.** The pilot chooses *what to cast*. Forge's AI still chooses
   blocks, attacks, responses and cost payments, and it doesn't know the
   pilot's plan. Engine plays that spend mana at sorcery speed leave Forge's
   defensive logic worse off, and the pilot never gets to cash in with combat.
2. **Intent doesn't flow into sub-decisions.** When the pilot activates Claws of
   Gix to recycle a spent saga, Forge's cost logic picks what to sacrifice.
   Mandatory-mode targets are Forge's too.
3. **Board state lacks card text** for opponents' permanents, so Jev knows names
   and power/toughness but not what a threat does.
4. **Sample size.** 12 games cannot resolve a 10–15 point difference. None of the
   win-rate gaps is statistically meaningful (p = 0.22 and 0.59).

## What would make it worth it

Give the pilot the decisions that decide games, with intent carried through:
- **Attacks:** one Choice per creature (attack whom / hold), or a Choice among
  a few whole-board attack plans that Forge proposes.
- **Blocks:** one Choice per incoming attacker.
- **Costs and targets:** when the pilot picks a play, it also picks its
  sacrifice and targets, from Forge's legal lists, as Choice questions in the
  same Jev request (speculative fan-out).
- **Instant-speed windows:** at an opponent's end step and in response to a
  bomb, a hold-or-fire question.
- **Board text:** oracle text for any opposing permanent the memo names as a
  threat.

Then measure with more games (≥100 per arm; ~6 hours on 3 workers with the
strategist), or with a sharper test: the same deck with pilot vs without in the
same pod.

## Engineering notes

- Forge sims hold no game clock of their own beyond `-c`. Forge's AI can spend
  tens of minutes evaluating removal targets on a very large board (combat
  prediction per candidate). The pilot clock is capped at 15 minutes so such
  games become draws.
- The headless strategist must run from a neutral directory with `--tools ""`,
  `--system-prompt` and `--effort low`. Otherwise `claude -p` loads the whole
  project harness and a memo takes about 2 minutes instead of about 10 s.
- Forge's AI evaluation isn't free. Evaluating every legal play each decision
  (so declined plays can be offered) adds about 0.1–0.5 s per decision on big
  boards.
